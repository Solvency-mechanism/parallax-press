Rural California: Water, Air, and Health in the San Joaquin Valley

A Common Knowledge Project collection

Who carries the health and environmental costs that do not appear in the price of what the Valley produces?

Scope and approach

This first installment of the Rural California collection focuses on the San Joaquin Valley. It does not describe all of rural California or assume the same conditions across the Imperial Valley, Sierra foothills, and other regions.

Start with the field guide, then follow the reference entries to examine its concepts, evidence, and limits. Studies describe particular places, populations, and periods. Exposure is not a diagnosis; regional disparities do not by themselves establish responsibility for an individual household’s water or health.

The collection asks how burdens are distributed and how existing public programmes and producer obligations address them. It does not provide a complete accounting of agricultural costs or establish an agriculture-attributable Medi-Cal total.

Suggested reading order

  1. Start here: The Bill Comes Due in the Body - Health and Environmental Costs in the San Joaquin Valley

    The field guide connects water, air, and pesticide evidence with the question of who carries the costs. It includes listening questions, claims to avoid, and routes to further information.

  2. Follow the costs: Externalization

    A framework for distinguishing costs borne by producers from burdens shifted to households, workers, and the public.

  3. Understand the institutions: Disadvantaged Unincorporated Communities

    How community boundaries, service providers, infrastructure, and public decision-making shape access to basic services.

  4. Examine drinking water: Nitrate and Arsenic Contamination in Disadvantaged Communities

    Different contaminants, different pathways: what drinking-water studies show, and what regional findings cannot establish about an individual well.

  5. Examine public responses: Human Right to Water

    California’s statutory policy, implementation programmes, and the difference between a recognized right and reliable, affordable service.

  6. Examine pesticide exposure: Pesticide Exposure in the San Joaquin Valley

    What personal-air sampling establishes about exposure, with limits on population coverage and conclusions about health effects.

  7. Examine the air: San Joaquin Valley Air Quality

    Pollutants, multiple sources, geography, and health evidence, with limits on attributing the Valley’s air burden to agriculture.

Using this collection

The field guide is an interpretation assembled for community organizers, canvassers, and interested residents. The six reference entries provide factual foundations and source links. Their cited studies and official resources allow readers to inspect the evidence and pursue more specific questions.

For practical information about drinking water, air conditions, and pesticide reporting, start with the field guide’s information and assistance resources. They are entry points rather than a substitute for sampling, medical care, or site-specific advice.

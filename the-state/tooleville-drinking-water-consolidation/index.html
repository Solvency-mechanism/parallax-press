Tooleville Drinking Water Consolidation

From The Common Knowledge Project, the free reference archive

Exeter declined to connect Tooleville in 2019; later records show an agreement and funded planning, but not yet permanent service.

What it is

Tooleville's proposed connection and consolidation with Exeter supplies a documented historical blockage followed by resumed development, not evidence of a project permanently stalled as of October 2026. Reporting records Exeter's September 2019 refusal; later city and state records show agreement and funded planning. The October 2026 city tracker marks a preparatory subproject completed, not the permanent consolidation. IRW, The Great Divide, "Wishful thinking"; Exeter project tracker, rows 7 and 12.

Why it matters

The case tests an account that treats agreement, state intervention, or funding as equivalent to service. It also prevents a comparison with East Orosi Drinking Water Consolidation and Matheny Tract Drinking Water Consolidation from freezing one community into a permanent "failure" category. Historical blockage and later progress can both be true. The interpretive comparison is in From the Right to Water to Water at the Tap.

How it works in practice

A recorded refusal

Investigative Reporting Workshop's original field reporting describes residents and legal advocates addressing Exeter on September 10, 2019, followed by a unanimous vote to take no action. It also records the mayor's stated priority of securing Exeter's existing supply and affordability before expansion. Advocates' accounts of state willingness to fund improvements were proposals or assurances, not an inspected executed grant. The council's own draft action minutes confirm the unanimous motion "to take no action," and record Leadership Counsel's report that the State had shown a willingness, but not a commitment, to discuss financial incentives for the city. This establishes an affirmative institutional refusal, rather than inferring a stall from missing completion records. Ramsey, The Great Divide, "Wishful thinking," published 2020 with a separately marked 2022 update; Exeter council packet, September 24, 2019, item H1, PDF p. 2.

These are different funding instruments and scopes; their amounts are not summed into a total project budget. The tracker's March 10, 2026 date is the council approval date, not a reported completion date. A separate $1.56 million row, a State Water Board funding-agreement authorization approved December 9, 2025, remains at "Preparing RFP." That row does not name Tooleville; matching it to the Board's Tooleville agreement rests on the same amount and timing. The public tracker cautions that it does not replace underlying agendas and minutes.

Signed agreement terms, permanent scheme acceptance, permit and household outcomes remain open. Sources were accessed on 2026-10-19.

What they say vs. what's true

Common claims and the record against them.
What they sayWhat's true

Accounts of Tooleville tend to stop at one moment: either the 2019 refusal, as if the project were permanently stalled, or a later funding or agreement announcement, as if the service problem were already solved.

The inspected record supports historical refusal, subsequent agreement, planning finance, and a completed preparatory task. It does not establish completed permanent connection, present household water quality, reliable supply, affordable bills, or the independent causal effect of resident advocacy. This is a stalled-episode/resumption comparator, not a currently inert-project comparator.

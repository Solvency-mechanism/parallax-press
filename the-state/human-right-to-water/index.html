What it is

California's AB 685 established safe, clean, affordable, accessible water for drinking, cooking, and sanitation as a human right recognized in state policy. Water Code section 106.3(b) requires relevant state agencies to consider that policy when revising, adopting, or establishing pertinent policies, regulations, and grant criteria. This note concerns California's statutory formulation rather than every international account of the right. AB 685, section 1(a)-(b).

The statute contains express limits. Subsection (c) does not expand state water-provision or infrastructure-expenditure obligations beyond those associated with (b); (d) excludes supplies for new development; (e) preserves public water systems' rights and responsibilities. Recognition is distinct from an automatically funded household connection. AB 685, section 1(c)-(e).

Why it matters

For Disadvantaged Unincorporated Communities, the question is both what government recognizes and what institutions can deliver. The Water Board connects the policy to compliance records, financial assistance, and public water-quality information. These provide ways to compare commitments with conditions. Water Board, Human Right to Water.

Water quality is one dimension of the right. A technically compliant supply can still pose questions of affordability, reliability, and access. Assessing progress requires preserving those distinctions rather than treating construction spending, regulatory compliance, and residents' experience as interchangeable measures.

How it works in practice

SAFER combines funding, technical assistance, interim supplies, regulatory authorities, and community participation. Its stated approach favors voluntary collaboration while retaining regulatory options. Drinking Water System Consolidation and administrator appointments use powers distinct from AB 685's policy declaration. Water Board, About SAFER.

There is documented progress as well as unfinished work. At its March 12, 2026 advisory meeting, Water Board staff reported that 175 of the original 366 systems identified as failing in the 2019 cohort had returned to compliance. This is a dated statewide cohort measure, not a San Joaquin Valley total. Participants also discussed project delays, rate increases, and the need for community involvement. SAFER meeting notes, 2026.

East Orosi Drinking Water Consolidation provides a local way to follow the steps between recognition and service: regulatory action, project finance, construction, household connections, and operation. A project milestone does not by itself establish reliable, affordable water at every household.

For current screening, the Water Board's Needs Assessment separately identifies failing public systems, systems at risk, and risks affecting state small systems and domestic wells. Those categories describe different evidence and should not be added together as a count of households with confirmed unsafe tap water. Needs Assessment.

What they say vs. what's true

Common claims and the record against them.
What they sayWhat's true

The Water Board describes California as statutorily recognizing a human right to water.

The policy and consideration duty are real, with express limits. Describing AB 685 as an immediate guarantee of a funded connection exceeds its text. Continued unsafe supply also does not establish that recognition has had no institutional effects. The analytical question of how rights become services belongs in From the Right to Water to Water at the Tap.
